History · part 1

The inventors (1980s)

1981

Hideo Kodama, Japan

Writes about building a part one thin layer at a time, using light to harden liquid plastic.

1983

Chuck Hull prints the first part

His machine uses a UV laser on a vat of liquid resin. He calls it stereolithography (SLA).

1986

3D Systems and the .STL file

Hull starts a company. Its STL file format is the same kind of file we still send to our printer.

1988

Scott Crump and a glue gun

Making a toy frog for his daughter, he pipes hot plastic layer by layer. That's FDM, how our printers work.

History · part 2

Printers for everyone

2005

RepRap

Adrian Bowyer in England designs a free printer that can print many of its own parts.

2009

The patent runs out

Anyone can now build FDM printers. Small companies like MakerBot start selling kits.

2016–17

Creality CR-10

A huge 300 x 300 mm bed for about $400. My first printer.

2020s

Fast and smart

Printers like our Bambu P1P print several times faster and watch themselves with sensors.

Telling the machine what to do

Printer · G-code (from the slicer)

M109 S200   ; wait until it is hot
G28          ; go home (hit the switches)
G1 X50 Y20 ; move nozzle to x=50, y=20
G1 X80 E1.2 ; draw a line, push plastic
